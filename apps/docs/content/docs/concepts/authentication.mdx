## Ways to sign in

| Flow | Endpoint | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| Email + password | `POST /v1/identity/sign-up/email`, `POST /v1/identity/sign-in/email` | Owners, managers, agents |
| Phone + SMS code | `POST /v1/identity/otp/request`, `POST /v1/identity/otp/verify` | Caretakers, tenants, anyone without email |
| Password reset | `POST /v1/identity/password/forgot`, `POST /v1/identity/password/reset` | Email users |

Every sign-in answers with a `token`. Browsers also receive a session cookie; mobile apps send
`Authorization: Bearer <token>`. `POST /v1/identity/sign-out` ends the session.

<Callout title="Cross-site protection">
Cookie-authenticated writes must come from a trusted origin (the web app's own domain);
requests from other origins are refused with `403 origin_not_allowed`. Bearer clients are not
affected.
</Callout>

## Email verification

New email accounts get a verification link. Confirm with
`POST /v1/identity/email/verify {"token": "…"}`; resend with
`POST /v1/identity/email/verification`.

## Invitations

Owners and managers invite people by email or phone (`POST /v1/invitations`). The invitee sees
it under `GET /v1/identity/me/invitations` and accepts with
`POST /v1/invitations/{id}/accept`. An invitation is only visible to — and can only be accepted
by — someone who has **verified** the invited address: the invited phone number (by signing in
with its SMS code) or the invited email (by confirming it).

## Rate limits

Sign-in, sign-up, codes, resets and verification are rate-limited per address and per IP. A
`429` response says how long to wait.
